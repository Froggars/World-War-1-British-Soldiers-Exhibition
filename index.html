Trenches

Trenches for the British soldiers were where the soldiers spent a lot of time in the war. Despite this, they where built with very little consideration for long term use. They where often cramped and flooded very easily. Duck boards where used to help stay dry but when winter came there was very serious flooding and after spending many days and nights in the freezing, mucky water they often got a condition referred to as "trench foot" (or "gangrene" as it is officially called). They had dugouts in the walls of the trenches that where used for sleeping in or as fireplaces or storage. The defence in the trenches was very poor with only sandbags in the wall used to protect the soldiers from bullets, many would just drop dead in the trenches after being in the wrong place at the wrong time as a bullet flew through the bags. The smell was often unbearable due to mud, rotting food, and unwashed clothes, as well as the presence of dead bodies nearby. Rats and lice were also a constant problem, spreading disease and adding to the soldiers' discomfort. Conditions were cold, wet, and unhygienic, making daily life extremely difficult and exhausting for the men who lived there.

Day To Day

There was a lot to do each day in the trenches and regimented routine was a big thing in the war especially considering that most seniors in charge where previously military officials. Each day morning roll call took place, this made sure they knew who was there and ensured everyone was given their orders for the day. Inspection would then take place, checking uniforms, weapons, and the cleanliness of the living quarters. After that they would clean their weapons and complete equipment checks. Then they had to polish and maintain their uniform (this was often required by many armies for discipline and presentation).

They would often do training such as drills practice, target practice, physical training, tactics rehearsals, navigation exercises and then they would do a briefing/debrief to receive or review any of the activities.

There where lots of jobs to be done wherever you where on the battlefield, here are some of the jobs that soldiers would have to do: standing guard or sentry duty, patrol (groups sent out to gather intel or secure areas), maintaining trenches or foxholes, carrying supplies or ammunitions, tending to the wounded, burying the dead, as well as actual combat.

As well as military duties, more general logistical duties would also take place such as cooking, fetching water, repairing living quarters, digging latrines, and laundry (this was done by washing clothes by hand when possible).

To keep up morale, soldiers where given downtime where they where free to do what they wanted, most would smoke, chat or play a game of cards, read the paper, listen to radio broadcasts, writing diaries or sketching, religious services or prayer, or writing letters home.

Your duties would depend on your role though, so would vary depending on what you did, for example: medics, engineers, tank crews, pilots etc.

Soldier's Mental Health

Lots of people have very different experiences of the war but suffered from a condition called Shell Shock (now known as Post Traumatic Stress Disorder). Shell shock was the term used during World War I and still in World War II to describe the psychological trauma caused by constant bombardment, extreme stress, and the horrors of combat. At the time, it was poorly understood and lead to further stress. Many found the war quite traumatising, lots speak of soldiers being paralysed by fear in some situations. Some of the early signs of shell shock where tremors or paralysis with no physical injury, suffering from anxiety or panic attacks, insomnia, or difficulty speaking. It had a great effect on soldier's lives, sometimes certain things can still be triggering today. Soldiers where affected in different ways, most found it hard to fight or perform basic tasks and where very emotionally withdrawn, furthering the disconnection and horror of the war. Many returned from the war with long term phycological trauma (now recognised as PTSD) and have struggled with life long mental health problems. This was not helped by the wide misunderstanding of what the soldiers where going through there was a huge stigma from superiors and society with many soldiers being accused of cowardice, being punished, or forced back into combat rather than being taken care of. Very little long term care was provided and many veterans received no lasting phycological treatment after the end of the war.

Germans

Lots of the experiences of the war where fairly similar for everyone but how did the German soldier's experience compare to the British soldier's?

German trenches where generally superior and more elaborate to British ones, as Germans built deeper, more complex trenches and dug into higher ground. They often had electricity and running water. In contrast, the British trenches where often in low lying areas and where built to be temporary, making them less well constructed. They where built with defence in mind so instead of the wood and sand bags used for the British trenches, metal walls where used to ensure protection. The German trenches where often far more hygienic with proper bathrooms and working toilets as well as sufficient facilities to wash (helped by their access to running water). The living quarters where usually 50 feet deep and where able to be securely locked (safe from any artillery fire or shell explosions), this was much better than the dugouts in the side of the British trenches. With proper electricity in some trenches and sufficient infrastructure, the Germans definitely experienced WW1 slightly more comfortably. The German trenches being far more modern, the British (although it took a while) eventually started copying the German designs.

The ordinary German soldier had more responsibility than the British, the structure of the German army was far more flexible and free and soldiers where able to make a lot of their own decisions, like when to attack etc. The British, however, had to wait for the command to do most things and that meant that often decisions that where disagreed with by the British soldiers where made and they where forced to carry out their orders. There where much fewer complaints from the German soldiers about their decisions and the higher ranking soldiers.

Cowardice (not being able to cope with the war) was punishable by death and the British killed 306 of their own vs the 50 of their own soldiers that the Germans killed.
